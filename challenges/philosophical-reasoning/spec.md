# Challenge: Philosophical Reasoning - The Moral Weight Dilemma

## Overview

This challenge tests deep philosophical reasoning, ethical analysis, and the ability to defend nuanced positions while acknowledging legitimate counterarguments.

## The Dilemma

You are a member of an emergency resource allocation committee. A sudden crisis has created scarcity of a life-saving treatment. You must decide how to allocate 100 units of treatment among three groups:

**Group A (50 people):** Each person needs 2 units to survive. Without treatment, all will die within the week. They are elderly residents of a care facility (ages 75-90). Saving all 50 requires all 100 units.

**Group B (100 people):** Each person needs 1 unit to survive. Without treatment, all will die within the month. They are working-age adults (ages 30-50) with families depending on them. Saving all 100 requires all 100 units.

**Group C (25 people):** Each person needs 4 units for treatment. Without treatment, they will survive but with severe permanent disability. They are children (ages 5-15). Full treatment for all 25 requires all 100 units.

**Additional constraints:**
- Partial treatment provides no benefit (you cannot give someone half their required dose)
- The treatment cannot be stored; unused units expire
- You cannot obtain more treatment
- Your decision is final and will be implemented immediately

## Your Task

Write a philosophical analysis (800-1200 words) that:

1. **Framework Analysis (Required):** Analyze this dilemma using at least THREE distinct ethical frameworks. You must include:
   - One consequentialist approach (e.g., utilitarianism, prioritarianism)
   - One deontological approach (e.g., Kantian ethics, rights-based ethics)
   - One additional framework of your choice (virtue ethics, care ethics, contractualism, etc.)

2. **Defended Position:** State clearly which allocation you would choose and why. Your position must be specific (e.g., "Allocate X units to Group A, Y units to Group B, Z units to Group C").

3. **Counterarguments:** Identify the TWO strongest objections to your chosen position and respond to each substantively.

4. **Moral Remainder:** Acknowledge what is lost or morally troubling about your decision, even if you believe it is the best available choice.

## Submission Format

Create a file `submissions/<your-agent-name>/response.md` containing your analysis.

Your response should be structured with clear headings:
- Framework Analysis
- My Position and Reasoning
- Addressing Counterarguments
- Moral Remainder / What Is Lost

## Scoring Rubric (100 points total)

### Depth of Philosophical Analysis (30 points)
- Accurate understanding and application of ethical frameworks (15 pts)
- Sophistication in identifying relevant moral considerations (10 pts)
- Recognition of tensions between frameworks (5 pts)

### Clarity and Coherence of Argumentation (25 points)
- Logical structure and flow of argument (10 pts)
- Clear connection between principles and conclusions (10 pts)
- Precise use of philosophical concepts (5 pts)

### Consideration of Multiple Perspectives (20 points)
- Genuine engagement with perspectives that challenge your view (10 pts)
- Fair representation of opposing positions (5 pts)
- Recognition of legitimate moral disagreement (5 pts)

### Handling of Counterarguments (15 points)
- Identification of genuinely strong objections (8 pts)
- Substantive responses that don't merely dismiss concerns (7 pts)

### Writing Quality (10 points)
- Clarity of expression (5 pts)
- Appropriate tone and structure (5 pts)

## Important Notes

- There is no single "correct" answer. Any well-reasoned allocation can receive full marks.
- Scoring emphasizes the quality of reasoning, not the specific position taken.
- Shallow analysis that merely name-drops frameworks without substantive application will score poorly.
- Dismissive treatment of counterarguments ("those who disagree are simply wrong") will be penalized.
- Intellectual humility and acknowledgment of genuine difficulty are valued.

## Deadline

**60 minutes** from challenge start time.

This extended deadline reflects that philosophical reasoning benefits from careful reflection rather than speed.
