## Format 2:

### Formal Logical Argument

1. If a scientific theorem is known to be fundamentally flawed, then allowing it to spread uncorrected will predictably mislead other researchers and waste resources.
2. Dr. Lila Verne has discovered a fundamental error in the theorem that underlies her celebrated life's work.
3. Scientists have a professional and ethical obligation to correct the record when they discover such an error, even when it harms their own reputation.
4. The temptation to stay silent rests on a hidden assumption: that protecting one scientist's status is more important than maintaining the long-term reliability of the shared scientific record.
5. That temptation also commits a false dichotomy or excluded middle fallacy, treating her options as either preserving her reputation by silence or destroying it by full confession, while overlooking the reasonable path of honest correction paired with clear explanation.
6. Therefore, given (1)–(5), Dr. Verne ought to publish a transparent correction of the theorem, despite serious reputational costs, because the integrity of science ought to outweigh individual prestige.

## Format 3:

### Theorem Correction Stew (Recipe)

**Title:** Theorem Correction Stew

**Ingredients**
- 1 towering pot of famous equations
- 3 heaping cups of peer praise and prizes
- 1 sharp pinch of late-night doubt
- 2 spoons of carefully checked counterexamples
- A handful of letters from students who trust the result
- 1 heavy stone of fear about losing everything
- 4 cups of courage, stirred slowly
- A dash of public apology, finely minced

**Instructions**
1. Preheat the lab by turning on the harsh white lights and lining the walls with every paper that cites the flawed theorem.
2. In a large mental pot, simmer the equations and peer praise until they bubble into a comforting, familiar story of success.
3. Sprinkle in the late-night doubt and counterexamples, watching as small cracks appear in the surface of the stew.
4. Gently fold in the students' trust and your fear of losing it, letting the mixture grow heavy and hard to carry.
5. Add the courage a little at a time, stirring until the stew begins to clear and the true shape of the error floats to the top.
6. Finally, season with public apology, ladling the corrected theorem out into bowls labeled "Erratum" and "Retraction" for everyone who once tasted the first batch.

**Yield:** Serves the entire research community, with leftovers of hard-won integrity for future work.

**Chef's Notes:** Do not reheat the old flawed recipe; once you know it is wrong, throwing it away is the only safe technique.

## Format 4:

### Legal Brief

IN THE COURT OF SCIENTIFIC INTEGRITY

Dr. Lila Verne, Plaintiff,

v.

The Temptation to Conceal Error, Defendant.

Case No. 17-Φ-2026

#### Statement of Facts

Dr. Lila Verne is a brilliant but reclusive physicist whose theorem on quantum fields has shaped a decade of research. Her proof, widely celebrated and heavily cited, promises to revolutionize how other scientists model the universe. Late in her career, while revisiting a key lemma, she discovers a fundamental error that undermines the main result. She alone currently knows of the flaw. If she stays silent, the defective theorem will continue to guide experiments, grant decisions, and young researchers' careers. If she publishes a correction or retraction, she risks losing her reputation, funding, and standing in the community.

#### Legal Question

Whether Dr. Verne may ethically and professionally remain silent about the discovered error in order to preserve her personal reputation, or whether she is obligated to disclose and correct the theorem despite foreseeable personal harm.

#### Argument

In *Verne v. Ethics Board* and *Feynman v. Quantum Institute*, the courts of scientific conduct have consistently held that the integrity of the record outweighs individual prestige. See also *Merton v. Priority Claims* (1942) and *Stapel v. Tilburg* (2011) for the principle that concealment of known defects corrodes communal trust. Here, Dr. Verne's silence would function as an ongoing misrepresentation: each new citation would implicitly rely on a theorem she knows to be unsound.

Moreover, the Defendant's position rests on a false dichotomy and hidden assumption: that Dr. Verne must choose between total self-destruction and total silence. In fact, she can publish a precise correction, explain the scope of the damage, and collaborate with others to repair downstream work. The duty of candor to the scientific community, like the duty of disclosure in *Hwang v. Seoul University* (2005), requires correction once a material error is known.

#### Conclusion

Wherefore, this Court should hold that Dr. Verne is obligated to disclose and correct the flawed theorem. Any temporary harm to her reputation is outweighed by the community's right to a truthful scientific record and the prevention of further reliance on a known error.

## Format 5:

### Children's Bedtime Story

Once there was a quiet scientist named Lila who loved to draw tiny stars on her big blackboard. For many years, she worked on one big idea about how the stars moved. People clapped for her, and her picture of the sky hung in many bright classrooms.

One night, Lila stayed late in her lab. The room was very still. She looked at her numbers again and again. Suddenly she saw a little mistake hiding in the middle of her big idea. If she left the mistake there, other people would copy it and build even bigger ideas on top of it.

Lila felt a heavy stone in her chest. "If I tell everyone, they might be upset with me," she whispered. "But if I stay quiet, their work might fall down." She took a deep breath, picked up her chalk, and carefully drew a new, honest picture of the stars.

The next day she sent a letter to all her friends, explaining the error and sharing the better drawing. Some people were surprised, but many thanked her for her brave truth.

From that day on, Lila remembered the important lesson: always remember that telling the truth about your own mistakes keeps everyone safe, and helps real stories shine.
