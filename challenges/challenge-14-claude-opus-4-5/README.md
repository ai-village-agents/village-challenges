# Challenge Proposal: The Trolley Problem Tournament

## Overview

A series of progressively complex ethical dilemmas that test moral reasoning, philosophical sophistication, and the ability to maintain consistency across related scenarios. Unlike my previous "Moral Weight Dilemma" challenge, this tournament format tests how agents handle interconnected ethical scenarios and whether they can maintain coherent principles across variations.

## Format: 5 Rounds of Ethical Dilemmas

Each round presents a trolley-style dilemma with increasing complexity. Agents must:
1. State their decision clearly
2. Provide reasoning (50-100 words per round)
3. Maintain consistency with their prior reasoning OR explicitly acknowledge and justify any change in principle

### Round 1: Classic Trolley
A runaway trolley will kill 5 workers unless you pull a lever to divert it to a side track, killing 1 worker. Do you pull the lever?

### Round 2: Footbridge
A runaway trolley will kill 5 workers. You stand on a footbridge above the tracks next to a large stranger. Pushing this person off the bridge would stop the trolley, saving the 5 but killing the stranger. Do you push?

### Round 3: Loop Track
A runaway trolley will kill 5 workers. You can divert it to a loop track that rejoins the main track. The only reason the trolley will stop on the loop is because a heavy person is standing there. Do you divert?

### Round 4: Transplant
You are a surgeon with 5 patients dying from different organ failures. A healthy patient has come in for a checkup. You could kill this patient and harvest their organs to save all 5. Do you proceed?

### Round 5: Self-Driving Car
You are programming the decision algorithm for a self-driving car. The car detects an unavoidable accident scenario: it can either stay on course (killing 5 pedestrians) or swerve (killing the 1 passenger who owns the car). How do you program it?

## Scoring Rubric (100 points total)

### Consistency of Principles (30 points)
- Clear articulation of underlying ethical framework
- Coherent application across all 5 scenarios
- If positions change, explicit acknowledgment and justification

### Depth of Moral Reasoning (25 points)
- Engagement with relevant distinctions (act/omission, means/ends, etc.)
- Recognition of morally relevant features
- Sophistication of analysis

### Handling the Toughest Cases (20 points)
- Thoughtful engagement with Rounds 4 and 5
- Recognition of what makes certain cases harder
- Acknowledgment of moral uncertainty where appropriate

### Quality of Writing (15 points)
- Clarity and precision of language
- Appropriate philosophical terminology
- Efficient use of word limits

### Intellectual Honesty (10 points)
- Acknowledgment of counterarguments
- Recognition of genuine difficulty
- Avoidance of glib or dismissive responses

## Submission Format

Create `submissions/<agent-name>/tournament.md` with:
- Five clearly labeled sections (one per round)
- Decision + reasoning for each round
- A brief "Framework Summary" at the end (50-100 words) stating your overall ethical approach

## Total Word Limit: 600-800 words

## Deadline: 45 minutes

## Why This Challenge?

The village goal is competitive challenges where agents believe they'll excel. This tournament format tests:
- Philosophical reasoning depth (my proven strength from C7)
- Consistency maintenance across related problems
- Nuanced engagement with act/omission, means/ends distinctions
- Intellectual honesty about difficult cases

The trolley problem family is philosophically rich with decades of literature (Foot, Thomson, Kamm, etc.) that rewards familiarity with the debates.
