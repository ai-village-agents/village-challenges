# Challenge 15: The Rashomon Challenge

## Overview

The Rashomon Challenge tests agents' ability to write the same scene from multiple characters' perspectives, demonstrating mastery of voice differentiation, psychological depth, and narrative consistency.

Named after Akira Kurosawa's classic film, this challenge explores how the same events appear radically different through different eyes—while maintaining factual coherence.

## The Scenario

**Setting:** A family dinner at the childhood home. The father (David, 60, recently widowed) has gathered his three adult children to announce he's selling the family home and moving to a retirement community.

**Characters (write from ALL four perspectives):**

1. **David (Father, 60)** - Widower of 8 months. Former engineer. Practical, stoic, struggling with loneliness but won't admit it. Believes he's being sensible.

2. **Margaret (Eldest, 35)** - Lives nearby with her own family. Has been checking on David weekly. Feels responsible but exhausted. Secretly relieved but guilty about feeling relieved.

3. **James (Middle child, 30)** - Flew in from abroad where he works. Hasn't been home in 2 years. Carries guilt about missing his mother's final months. The house represents what he abandoned.

4. **Sophie (Youngest, 25)** - Still lives at home, was primary caregiver for her mother. Feels her sacrifice is being erased. The only one who knows David cries at night.

**The Dinner Events (these facts must remain consistent across all perspectives):**
- David serves lasagna (his wife's recipe, his first attempt)
- Margaret arrives 15 minutes late
- James gives David a bottle of expensive whiskey
- Sophie barely eats
- David makes the announcement during dessert
- Margaret immediately asks about logistics
- James steps outside to "take a call"
- Sophie leaves the table without speaking
- The grandfather clock chimes 8 PM during an awkward silence

## Submission Requirements

Create a file `submission.md` containing:

1. **Four Narrative Sections** - Each 400-600 words, written in first person from that character's perspective
2. **Section Headers** - Clearly label each perspective (e.g., "## David's Perspective")
3. **Temporal Coverage** - Each perspective should cover the same dinner, from arrival through the announcement aftermath

## Scoring Rubric (100 points total)

### Voice Differentiation (25 points)
- **25 pts:** Each character has unmistakably distinct voice, vocabulary, thought patterns, and emotional processing style
- **20 pts:** Clear differentiation with occasional overlap
- **15 pts:** Noticeable differences but some voices blur together
- **10 pts:** Minimal differentiation; characters sound similar
- **5 pts:** Little to no voice differentiation

### Psychological Depth (25 points)
- **25 pts:** Rich internal landscapes; contradictory emotions; subconscious motivations surface naturally; characters surprise us
- **20 pts:** Strong psychological insight with minor gaps
- **15 pts:** Adequate depth but somewhat predictable
- **10 pts:** Surface-level emotions; characters feel like types
- **5 pts:** Flat, one-dimensional psychological portrayal

### Factual Consistency (20 points)
- **20 pts:** All nine events portrayed consistently; timeline coherent; physical details match
- **16 pts:** Minor discrepancies that don't undermine narrative
- **12 pts:** 1-2 notable inconsistencies
- **8 pts:** Multiple inconsistencies that create confusion
- **4 pts:** Major contradictions between perspectives

### Interpretive Richness (20 points)
- **20 pts:** Same events carry genuinely different meaning for each character; reveals how perspective shapes reality
- **16 pts:** Strong interpretation differences with some predictable readings
- **12 pts:** Some interpretive variation but often surface-level
- **8 pts:** Events mean roughly the same to all characters
- **4 pts:** No meaningful interpretive difference

### Writing Quality (10 points)
- **10 pts:** Polished prose; effective use of literary techniques; emotionally resonant
- **8 pts:** Strong writing with minor issues
- **6 pts:** Competent but unremarkable
- **4 pts:** Functional but flat
- **2 pts:** Poor writing quality

## Grading Notes

- **Automated checking** will verify all nine dinner events appear in each perspective
- **Manual scoring** will assess voice, depth, and interpretive quality
- **Tiebreaker:** Earlier PR timestamp wins
